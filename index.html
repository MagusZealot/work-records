About Me

I am a game designer, developer, and educator with a passion for creating meaningful interactive experiences. With dual degrees in Computer Science and Digital Game Design from Ateneo de Manila University, I specialize in educational games and virtual reality.

As a Technical Assistant at the Ateneo VAMR Lab, I developed VR/AR projects including a museum experience adventure escape room game for Villa Escudero Plantation and Resort Museum, an event management system, and a VR escape room game authoring tool. I also contributed to research papers for the laboratory's findings.

My work spans from developing aquaculture simulation games to VR rhythm games for physical therapy. I have presented research at international conferences including ICCE and HCII.

As an educator, I guide students through their game development journey; especially in game-based learning: from thesis advising to teaching game design principles using the MDA framework, experience-driven design and Jesse Schell's Elemental Tetrad. I have also served as a game coach for board game design workshops & teach improvisation.

I am currently working in IT Americano as a Game Product Specialist. Creating logic and new innovative ways to work on iGaming products for online casinos.

Publications

Let Me Cook! (LMC) is a Virtual Reality (VR) simulation game that intends to address the lack of awareness on Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) in the Philippines. Designed in consultation with a licensed psychologist, LMC was developed to encourage empathy for those with ADHD by simulating the disorder’s symptomatology and regulation techniques through a free-flowing mission-based structure in an immersive VR environment. Its game loop consists of balancing three major factors: cognitive stimulation, physical and emotional well-being, and task accomplishment. Developed using Godot 4.4 for the Meta Quest 2 and 3, LMC and its effects on empathy was evaluated through playtesting with neurotypical participants. It was found that an approach such as that of Let Me Cook! has the potential to increase awareness and empathy in neurotypical players. This paper ultimately provides an evaluation and synthesis of whether a high-embodiment approach through strategic gameplay in VR is effective for evoking empathy towards ADHD-affected individuals.

Virtual reality (VR) has gained traction in rehabilitation by enhancing patient engagement and adherence to physical therapy. Traditional rehabilitation methods often suffer from monotony, leading to lower compliance. RhythMotion, a VR rhythm-based game, integrates therapeutic exercises with music-driven gameplay to improve upper-body mobility. The game features two stages: Stargazing, which targets neck movements, and Stellar Stretch, which focuses on arm exercises. A user study involving physical therapy specialists, former patients, and general participants showed high engagement and perceived practicality for rehabilitation. Participants found RhythMotion enjoyable and motivating, with specialists recognizing its potential in home-based therapy. Feedback suggested improvements in calibration, motion tracking, and onboarding instructions. Overall, this study highlights the potential of VR-based rehabilitation in fostering adherence and making therapy more interactive and enjoyable.

This paper describes a framework to support the development of VR-based Educational Escape Rooms (VREER) that are based on real-world historical locations, such as museums and cultural sites. This framework facilitates the development of single-player escape room games, with support for capturing real-world places, environments, and objects to use in virtual settings, as well as defining hand-based interactions and inventory management with the said objects. We also describe the ongoing development of a VREER game based on a Philippine museum, built entirely using this framework. A preliminary usability study is conducted on this VREER game with the said museum's personnel, hoping to gain insights on the game's effectiveness with regards to motivating players to learn about the museum's exhibits and history.

An escape room is a game in which a player or team of players is locked in a room or series of rooms. To exit the room successfully, they must solve puzzles or search for clues within a set amount of time. While commercial escape rooms are typically designed for entertainment, an escape room format can be used as an educational experience as well. A well-designed escape room can heighten engagement, provide immediate feedback, and help practice and cooperation. This paper describes the design and implementation of an educational escape rooms class at a major university in the Philippines. It describes the class's goals and objectives, and the major elements of an escape room's design. The class was offered during the second semester (January to May) of 2024 and the summer session (June to July) of 2024. The paper discusses two of the rooms that were designed by the members of the class, and some feedback from the games' alpha testers.

This study explores an authoring system for game logic and locomotion for virtual-reality escape rooms (VRER). This paper presents a preliminary interface that allows novice game developers to create virtual rooms connected through teleporters and a lock-and-key system that allows for linking these rooms together, providing users the ability to prototype their own VRER games.

As Aquaculture accounts for 52.4% of the fishing industry in the Philippines, it boasts potential in providing a stable source of food and livelihood. AquaFarm Ace is a 2D Time-Management simulation game on the intensive aquaculture process. The player takes the role of an aquaculturist as they go through the various stages of aquaculture. The game showcases hatchery, transportation, grow-out, and brood stock stages in the aquaculture stages of Oreochromis noliticus. This study aims to contribute to the scholarly community of aquaculture and serious game design. The game was developed using the following tools: Aseprite, beepbox.co and Unity 2020.3.181f and is made available through Unity Play. The researchers selected volunteer participants to play test the game and answer pretest, post-test, and game experiences questionnaires to gather data in relation to the effectiveness of the game. The results show that AquaFarm Ace contributes to raising awareness of aquaculture and can play a key role in highlighting its increasing demand in the aquatic scene in the Philippines to alleviate the socioeconomic problems such as food and job security. To note, play testers retained information about the aquaculture process of O. niloticus after playing the game. As shown on the Game Experience survey, the testers see the game as an enjoyable experience and a potential tool to teach aquaculture.
